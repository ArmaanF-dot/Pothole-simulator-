# 150-word summary (submission form)

Southampton fills over 4,000 potholes a year, and in four of the last five years it put nothing into preventative
maintenance. A reactive repair costs £87.80 against £56.94 for a planned one.

RoadSense helps the highways officers who plan Southampton's weekly maintenance decide which streets to treat before
potholes form. It scores all 8,135 council road segments using DfT heavy-vehicle counts, bus routes, flood zones and
Open-Meteo freeze-thaw forecasts, explains every score, and ranks a weekly prevention list for an officer to approve.
It also triages public FixMyStreet reports into draft work orders. On real 2018-19 reports, its top 10% of roads
caught 42% of reported potholes, against 34% for ranking by last year's reports. Council repair records are not public, so they are
replaced by clearly labelled synthetic data.

The council or its contractor pays an annual licence. Every input is national open data, so a second city needs only
its boundary and its own repair records.
